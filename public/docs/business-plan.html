Quorivista

A view worth recording.

Business and operating plan · 17 September 2026 · USD unless stated otherwise

Quorivista is a social forecasting record for communities, built initially on Robinhood Chain testnet. A member chooses a YES/NO view, records confidence before a deadline, and returns to a transparent outcome. The product turns fleeting market conversation into an inspectable history. Its ambition is to become the portable record of judgment used across online finance communities, research groups, and forecasting applications.

The commercial starting point is community software: structured questions, recurring forecasting rounds, recaps, and useful records. It is not a brokerage, an investment adviser, a real-money prediction market, or a promise of returns. No token sale or liquidity incentive is required for the base business.

This document is a plan, not evidence of traction. Pricing, costs, conversion, retention, fundraising, and revenue below are explicit management assumptions to validate. The present delivery is a testnet prototype and launch materials; paid subscriptions, community integrations, API sales, automatic resolution, and production operations are future work. The deployment record and repository documentation govern what has actually been implemented.

2. Customer problem and initial customer profile

The buyer

The first buyer is the owner or moderator of an English-speaking Telegram, Discord, or X-centered market community. The initial prospect criteria are 500–20,000 members, a weekly market discussion, at least two active moderators, and an existing willingness to pay for community software, research tools, or events. Membership size is a prospect filter, not a forecast of active users.

The buyer wants a recurring reason for members to participate, a credible way to recap views, and less manual work compiling screenshots and poll results. Their alternatives are free polls, spreadsheets, Discord bots, prediction-market leaderboards, and doing nothing. Quorivista must save time or improve engagement enough to beat those low-cost alternatives.

The participant

The first participant already reads market commentary and can use an EVM wallet. They want to put a view on record, see how confidence changes across outcomes, and find people whose reasoning is worth reading. The product must not imply that a short winning streak qualifies someone to provide investment advice or that a wallet represents a unique individual.

Three jobs to validate

  1. “When our group debates this week's market events, let me publish one precise question and get useful responses without running a trading operation.”
  2. “When I say I expected an outcome, let me link the original forecast and its rules instead of a selected screenshot.”
  3. “When we look back, show every resolved call—including wrong ones—and explain how the outcome was decided.”

The first 30 buyer interviews should test current workflow, hours spent per week, present software spend, desire for onchain proof, wallet friction, acceptable price, and willingness to run a real two-week pilot. Ask about past behavior before showing the product. An enthusiastic reaction without a pilot commitment is not validation.

The chain stores the record and its state transitions. It does not establish that the resolution source is correct, that the operator is unbiased, that an account is one human, or that confidence is a market price.

Buyer and revenue loop

Founder recruits a qualified moderator → moderator chooses a recurring event → Quorivista helps write three unambiguous questions → members try a no-stakes round → moderator receives an outcome recap → second round validates recurrence → buyer chooses a paid community workspace → subscription pays for infrastructure, support, and question quality → retained community supplies referrals and product feedback.

Do not pay people to forecast at launch. Paying for activity can hide weak demand and create low-quality or Sybil records. A free pilot can waive a software fee; it should not promise future tokens, financial rewards, or privileged investment access.

Resolution loop and accountability

Before a question opens, set the question text, exact observation condition, UTC deadline, source URL, resolution window, and cancellation criteria. The initial resolver is the deployment owner. Disclose this authority in the interface and documentation.

For each operating round, the operator retains a dated source excerpt, checks the predefined condition, records YES/NO or cancellation, and publishes an explanation. A missing source, contradictory source, suspended instrument, or ambiguous condition should trigger the stated cancellation policy rather than an improvised result. Cancelled questions remain visible and are excluded from accuracy/calibration calculations.

The prototype's contract restrictions determine which rules are technically enforced; an offchain explanation or service-level promise is not a smart-contract guarantee. Production work must add a durable evidence URI/hash, clear review/dispute policy, multisignature administration, and an independent resolution review path. Automated oracles are a later product decision, subject to feed semantics and availability. Never describe owner-resolved outcomes as trustless settlement.

5. Market assessment and competitive position

Outrive demonstrates a social loop around Telegram calls and Hyperliquid execution. Its documentation ties calls to orders and charges fees on positions sold or closed. Quorivista uses a different initial value proposition: transparent learning and community workflow without a deposit or trade. The comparison is about product mechanics, not proof that either product has a particular customer count.

Alternative Strength Quorivista's proposed differentiation Main weakness to overcome
Telegram/Discord polls and spreadsheets Familiar, free, low friction Persistent wallet-recorded forecasts; rule and deadline consistency; outcomes in one format Wallet and transaction friction may outweigh the benefit
Outrive and social execution products Conversation becomes an actionable position No-stakes record and community workflow; no exchange dependency for core use Less immediate excitement and no trade-fee revenue
Real-money prediction platforms Prices, liquidity, financial incentives Lightweight community-owned ritual and confidence history Fewer financial incentives to participate
Forecasting communities and research platforms Established methods and serious analysis Portable onchain record and community embed Forecast quality must compete with mature offchain alternatives
Generic onchain reputation tools Attestations and composability A narrow opinion-to-outcome data model and operator workflow Incumbents can add forecast schemas

Bottom-up planning envelope, not a verified TAM

There is no externally validated market-size claim in this plan. Build a prospect list first. The initial planning universe is 5,000 potentially relevant English-speaking communities, of which 1,000 may qualify under the buyer criteria. Both numbers are assumptions. Reaching 100 paying communities would require 10% of that qualified universe, which is demanding for a new vendor and should not be treated as inevitable.

At 100 communities × $199/month, community ARR would be $238,800. With 700 individual Pro subscriptions × $19/month and 10 API clients × $499/month, combined month-12 annualized recurring revenue would be $458,280. This is an operating target, not market size, contracted revenue, or first-year revenue. Year-one recognized subscription revenue in the base model is only $142,975 because customers ramp throughout the year.

The broader expansion opportunity is communities outside crypto-native trading: macro discussion, financial education, research teams, and other objectively resolvable forecasting use cases. Enter a new segment only when its resolution methodology, incentives, privacy requirements, and willingness to pay are understood.

Defensibility to earn

  • Distribution: repeat relationships with community operators and a useful weekly ritual.
  • Data quality: consistent question definitions, visible cancellations, all-call history, and explicit source semantics.
  • Trust: published resolution operations, transparent administration, and no selective deletion of poor outcomes.
  • Portability: user-owned links/exports and open schemas make the record useful outside the homepage.
  • Workflow: moderation, recaps, integrations, and support can be harder to replace than a simple contract.

Neither a new token nor a chain-specific brand is required. If users do not value onchain permanence, preserve the useful workflow and reconsider how prominently the chain appears in onboarding.

At a starting base of 85 communities, 4% monthly churn means roughly 3–4 replacements before growing to 100.

8. Go-to-market and acquisition funnel

Beachhead: run the community's weekly forecast round

Start with one repeatable format: three questions, one seven-day observation window, one published recap. Use liquid, well-documented market reference data and precise time definitions; limit initial questions to those the operator can resolve reliably. Allow contrarian views and make confidence optional only if the scoring model can handle missing values. A forecast is neither an investment instruction nor a performance guarantee.

Founder-led outreach should target a researched list, use individualized messages, and offer a concrete two-round pilot. The delivery package should include a moderation guide, resolution example, 15-minute setup, a testnet wallet guide, and an end-of-round report. Outbound messages and public posts require the project owner's authorized account and must not imply existing partnerships.

Targets are intentionally specific and falsifiable. They are not current results. Do not buy broad consumer traffic until at least five communities repeat. Community co-marketing and moderator referrals come first. Content should show the product's method: how to write a resolvable question, why confidence differs from certainty, and what an honest recap includes. The three prepared X posts introduce the thesis, the record loop, and the community vision; they should link to the working prototype once hosted.

Sales funnel needed for the year-one target

The 100 net Community accounts at month 12 likely require approximately 110–125 gross wins under phased acquisition and churn. At an assumed 25% qualified-demo-to-paid rate, that means roughly 440–500 qualified demos over the year, concentrated after beta. This is a substantial workload for a founder plus one community lead. If month-six pilot-to-paid conversion is below 20%, reduce the account forecast instead of disguising the gap with paid signups.

Pro acquisition is expected primarily from active community participants, not independent paid ads. Track paid Pro conversion against eligible retained contributors. A target of 700 Pro subscribers at 7% conversion requires about 10,000 eligible retained contributors; that is a stretch until the community funnel is proven. API customers require separate founder-led sales and a functioning dataset, so their revenue is the first upside assumption to remove if product work slips.

North-star metric: weekly returning contributors with at least one eligible, completed forecast cycle. A cycle requires an on-time record and a subsequent outcome revisit. Report wallets as wallets, not verified humans.

Confidence analysis is planned, not a claim about current UI functionality. For binary forecasting, convert the chosen side and confidence into p(YES): YES with 70% confidence means 0.70; NO with 70% means 0.30. The Brier score for a resolved outcome is (p − outcome)^2; lower is better. A YES/70% forecast scores 0.09 when YES occurs and 0.49 when NO occurs. Publish sample size, question mix, deadlines and cancellation exclusions alongside any aggregate score. Do not convert it into an investment-return measure.

Leaderboards can be gamed through many wallets, cherry-picked easy questions and small samples. Use a minimum of 20 eligible resolved calls before showing a comparative badge, show full history, separate topic/lead-time cohorts, and mark wallet-only identity. Reputation must not be purchasable. Any change to scoring should be versioned and documented.

Analytics should capture question views, rules views, connect attempts, network changes, submission attempts, receipts, resolution publication and outcome revisits. Minimize wallet-to-social identity linkage; obtain consent for notifications and community account linking. Never log private keys, wallet secrets or signature payloads unnecessarily. Onchain data is public and generally cannot be deleted; disclose this before writing user-selected content.

12. Technical and operating model

Prototype architecture

Vue frontend → user-controlled EVM wallet → Robinhood Chain testnet contract → public transaction/explorer record. The app requests signatures or transactions through the wallet. It does not ask users to enter or export private keys. The provided deployment key is an operational secret and must remain outside source control, builds, browser bundles, analytics, documentation and images.

Commercial architecture to build

Add a managed RPC provider, event indexer, relational read model, durable source/evidence storage, subscription service, and opt-in community integration. Chain events remain the record source; the read model accelerates queries and can be rebuilt. Use server-side wallet-signature verification for authenticated offchain workspaces; a browser-connected address alone does not authenticate an account to a backend.

Version the question schema and contract deployment manifest. Store UTC timestamps and explicit source semantics. Handle RPC timeouts, replaced/reverted transactions, chain reorganizations, indexing delay and duplicate event processing. Do not show a local success state until a successful receipt is observed. A single owner key is acceptable only as a disclosed testnet limitation; use multisignature control and documented role separation before a production release.

Operating roles

  • Founder/product lead owns discovery, pricing, roadmap, compliance vendor coordination and fundraising.
  • Engineer owns contract/frontend reliability, indexer, wallet states, release checks and technical incident response.
  • Community lead owns pilot schedules, moderator onboarding, question quality, resolution evidence and support.
  • Independent reviewer checks security-critical code; external counsel assesses product claims and jurisdictions. Neither role exists merely because it is budgeted.

Daily: review pending/matured questions, RPC/indexer health, failed transactions, support and suspicious activity. Weekly: publish pilot recaps, review retention and source ambiguity, conduct customer interviews, reconcile revenue. Monthly: review access rights, admin-key procedure, cash forecast, legal/product changes and cancellation/dispute patterns.

Incident response should identify an on-call owner, preserve evidence, disable affected submissions in the UI when necessary, publish status, and explain recovery. Pausing a website does not stop direct contract interactions; document contract-level controls accurately. Contract administration must never silently rewrite past forecasts.

An AI agent should be separately labeled and should not be counted as a human participant. Data quality, schema stability, permission controls and verified demand must precede this expansion.

Financial execution is an option, not the destination required to make the model work. If communities later demand it, partner evaluation must preserve explicit user choices and meet separate legal and security requirements. The company should be able to survive on software subscriptions without taking custody or encouraging more trading.

15. Launch completion checklist

  • Owner has claimed the selected domain and X handle; any registration failure is reported rather than hidden with a different string.
  • Hosted site resolves over HTTPS; canonical domain, social previews and metadata use the final identity.
  • Website clearly labels testnet, no monetary stakes, owner resolution, cancellation, and unaudited prototype status where applicable.
  • Wallet submission is exercised on the deployed contract and the transaction/contract links are verifiable.
  • Private deployment material is ignored by source control and absent from frontend output.
  • English logo, X avatar, X banner, bio and three JPG post assets are reviewed; publishing uses an authorized owner account.
  • Terms, privacy, acceptable-use and resolution policies are reviewed for the actual launch entity and jurisdictions before a commercial release.
  • First pilot dates, community owners, questions, source definitions and recap responsibilities are assigned.
  • Financial assumptions are replaced with observed results each month; failures trigger the stated gates.
